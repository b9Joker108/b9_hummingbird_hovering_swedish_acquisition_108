Hej! It is fascinating to look back at Davidson’s 1960s perspective. While her core descriptions of the structures remain solid, archaeology has moved from "guessing the dates" to high-precision science.
Since you are looking to build your Swedish vocabulary, let’s look at the modern understanding of these megalithic structures using Swedish terminology.
1. Dös (The Dolmen)
Etymology: From the Old Norse dys, meaning a burial mound or stone heap.
Davidson mentioned these appeared in the 3rd millennium BCE. Modern kol-14-datering (radiocarbon dating) has pushed this back. We now know the Trattbägarkulturen (Funnelbeaker Culture) began building these around 3500–3300 f.v.t. (BCE).
 * Construct: A dös typically consists of a few upright stones (väggstenar) supporting a massive capstone (takblock).
 * The Shift: We no longer see these as "invader" tech. Modern DNA analysis and site stratigraphy suggest a local evolution of funerary rites, where simple earth graves grew into these permanent stone houses for the dead to mark territory.
2. Gånggrift (The Passage Grave)
Etymology: A compound of gång (passage/hallway) and grift (grave/tomb).
The gånggrift is the "bigger brother" of the dös. These became dominant around 3300–3000 f.v.t.
 * Design: Unlike the single-chamber dös, these have a distinct stone-lined passage leading into a larger central chamber.
 * Location: While Davidson noted they are "plentiful in Denmark," Sweden has a world-class concentration in Falköping, known as the Falbygdens gånggrifter. There are over 200 of them in a small area!
 * Modern Insight: We now view these as kollektivgravar (collective graves). Stable isotope analysis of teeth found inside shows that these weren't just for "chiefs," but for a specific segment of a settled farming society.
3. Hällkista (The Stone Cist)
Etymology: From häll (flat stone slab) and kista (chest/coffin).
As the Neolithic transitioned into the Bronze Age (ca. 2400–1500 f.v.t.), the massive megaliths went out of style, replaced by the hällkista.
 * Structure: These are rectangular "boxes" made of thinner stone slabs, often buried level with the ground or under a small röse (cairn).
 * Difference: Davidson’s era struggled with the "late Neolithic" transition. Today, we see the hällkista as a reflection of a more mobile society that was beginning to trade in metal.

**Summary of Modern Revisions**

| Concept | Davidson’s View (1967) | Modern View (2020s) |
|---|---|---|
| Origins | Diffusion from the Mediterranean. | Independent Atlantic/Nordic development. |
| Dating | Primarily 3rd millennium BCE. | Firmly established by 3500 BCE. |
