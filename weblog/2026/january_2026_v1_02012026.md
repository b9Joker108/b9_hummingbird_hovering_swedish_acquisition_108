- Louis Masreliez, born Adrien Louis Masreliez in 1748 in Paris, France, was a prominent French-Swedish artist known for pioneering neoclassical interior design and painting in Sweden during the Gustavian era, with works that blended French heritage and Swedish royal aesthetics.
- His family background reflects French artisanal roots, as the son of sculptor Adrien Masreliez, who moved to Sweden for royal commissions, highlighting cultural migration patterns in 18th-century Europe.
- Evidence suggests his contributions, such as the interiors of Gustav III's Pavilion and Tullgarn Palace, embody a fusion of Pompeian motifs and Raphael-inspired grotesques, making him a bridge between European neoclassicism and Swedish decorative arts.

**Biographical Narrative: Parallel Texts for Immersion**

Read the following biography in Engelska, then Svenska, to build reading comprehension. Key phrases are bolded for emphasis; translate them as practice.

**Engelska Version:** Louis Masreliez, born Adrien Louis Masreliez in Paris in 1748, arrived in Sweden at age five when his father, the French ornamental sculptor Adrien Masreliez, was hired for Stockholm Palace. This **ancestral migration** (förfäders migration) reflects 18th-century artisan movements. He studied at the Royal Swedish Academy of Fine Arts from age ten, apprenticing under Lorens Gottman. In 1769, he traveled to Paris, Bologna, and Rome for twelve years, immersing in neoclassicism with influences from Anton Raphael Mengs and Pompeian excavations. Returning in 1783, he became a member of the Academy, professor of art history in 1784, rector in 1802, and director in 1805. His works include the neoclassical interiors of **Gustav III's Pavilion** at Haga Park (1792), Tullgarn Palace (1790s), and altarpieces like the Adoration of the Shepherds at Maria Magdalena Church (1800). Masreliez pioneered the late Gustavian style, blending French elegance with Swedish restraint. He died in Stockholm on 19 March 1810, leaving a legacy in museums like Nationalmuseum.

**Svenska Version:** Adrien Louis Masreliez, född 1748 i Paris, kom till Sverige vid fem års ålder när hans far, den franska ornamentsbildhuggaren Adrien Masreliez, anlitades för Stockholms slott. Denna **förfäders migration** speglar 1700-talets hantverksrörelser. Han studerade vid Kungliga Akademien för de fria konsterna från tio års ålder, i lärling hos Lorens Gottman. År 1769 reste han till Paris, Bologna och Rom i tolv år, och fördjupade sig i nyklassicism med influenser från Anton Raphael Mengs och pompejanska utgrävningar. Vid återkomsten 1783 blev han ledamot av Akademien, professor i konsthistoria 1784, rektor 1802 och direktör 1805. Hans verk inkluderar nyklassiska inredningar i **Gustav III:s paviljong** vid Hagaparken (1792), Tullgarns slott (1790-talet), och altartavlor som Herdarnas tillbedjan vid Maria Magdalena kyrka (1800). Masreliez var pionjär för den sengustavianska stilen, som blandade fransk elegans med svensk återhållsamhet. Han dog i Stockholm den 19 mars 1810 och lämnade ett arv i museer som Nationalmuseum.
